This file exists so the later standard is built from what the first two research runs needed, not from taste.

## What was actually checked

Everything in this section was verified against the named source. Nothing here is a guess.

A thin convention with a declared authority and a declared expiry was enough to break the loop in which research cannot be written until documentation rules exist, and documentation rules cannot be written until research exists. The governing rules were written under the bootstrap window's authority. Research became writable the moment those rules existed. No heavier method was needed to start. That matters in this estate because the first research had to be written before a finished standard existed. Checked in `AGENTS.md` and in the bootstrap record in `operations.md`.

The five things already asked for — the question, the sources, the split between checked facts and believed claims, confidence, and the date — each did real work in the first two research files. Both runs began from a one-sentence question, and every source decision was made against that sentence. Every section of both files exists because a requirement forced it. No required section turned out to be filler. That matters here because the later standard should not drop a field that already earned its keep. Checked against the writing of this file and of the documentation-practice research.

The split between checked facts and believed claims can be enforced sentence by sentence, and it changes how the writing is done. In the first runs it forced source checks that would not otherwise have happened: two external sources were checked live before they were cited, specifically because an unchecked statement would have had to be marked as a claim. That matters here because an unchecked citation in this repo would otherwise look as solid as a file someone just opened. Checked against those same first runs.

Once a research file lands, it is a closed dated record under the estate's persistence classes. It states what was known as of its date. A later file supersedes it. It is never edited into a different claim. The "re-grade, never erase" rule matches that. That matters here because later design will cite these files as they stood on their date. Checked in `AGENTS.md` (Truth).

Whether a source can be found again is a yes-or-no test this estate can run. Anything which cannot be found again is a claim, not a source. In the first runs that test sorted inputs cleanly into three piles: estate files (paths), external documents (URLs, checked on a date), and inference. That matters here because a source the next agent cannot open is not a source. Checked in `AGENTS.md` (Writing) and in the first runs.

Delegation economics apply to research. Once the question is fixed, gathering is output-heavy and mechanical. Synthesis is judgment. The estate's tiering procedure in `AGENTS.md` (Delegation) already distinguishes exactly those two shapes of work. That matters here because this estate already prices those two shapes differently, and research has both. Checked in `AGENTS.md` (Delegation).

The mature evidence-synthesis discipline makes a published protocol mandatory before a review begins. The protocol must carry predefined, unambiguous eligibility criteria. Searches are then motivated by those criteria. The stated purpose is to stop people from changing the rules after they see the data. That matters here because an agent in this repo that starts reading and then decides what counts will tilt toward a preferred answer. Checked in the Cochrane Handbook and the MECIR protocol-stage standards.

PRISMA 2020 is a 27-item reporting checklist. Its stated aim is a transparent, complete account of why a review was done, what was done, and what was found. It treats reporting as a discipline separate from conducting the review. That matters here because a file in this repo that only records conclusions hides the work, and a file that only records the work hides the answer. Checked at the PRISMA 2020 checklist.

Systematic-review method has been deliberately adapted from medicine to software engineering as a trustworthy, rigorous, auditable process in three phases: planning, conducting, and reporting. That shows the method transfers across domains when it is adapted rather than copied. That matters here because this estate is not a medical journal, so adaptation is the relevant result, not a wholesale copy. Checked in Kitchenham and Charters, EBSE-2007-01.

In software-engineering reviews, GRADE-style certainty assessment is the most used approach. It is usually misapplied as one generic grade for all findings. The method itself calls for a rating per finding. That is external confirmation that rating each claim is the documented standard, and rating only the file is the documented failure. That matters here because a design decision in this repo may rest on the one weak claim a file-level grade would hide. Checked in the 2025 Empirical Software Engineering paper on GRADE as applied in software engineering.

Both first runs put checked findings in one part of the file and implications in another, without being told to. The documentation-practice run produced one closed question of the form "not worth the machinery yet": whether to declare a Diátaxis mode per document. `AGENTS.md` had no place for that result other than a rated claim. Checked against those runs.

## What I believe follows

Everything in this section is believed or inferred. Each point says how sure I am and why.

I am sure the question must be fixed before gathering starts. Research that starts from sources instead inherits the sources' scope. That is the single strongest candidate for the standard's first requirement. I am sure because both first runs worked that way, and because a source-led start would have imported the sources' scope into this repo's research.

I am sure the question, the eligibility of sources, and the stopping conditions should all be fixed before gathering starts, so inclusion rules cannot drift toward a preferred conclusion. For a lookup, one line each is enough. For anything deeper, a short protocol note inside the research file. The value is the constraint stated in advance. Ceremony is not. I am sure because the Cochrane and MECIR sources make that constraint mandatory, and the first runs already needed the question fixed first.

I am sure different kinds of sources deserve different checks. This estate's own files are checkable by reading them. External documents need a reachability-and-characterization check on a date. Memory or inference must be labeled a claim no matter how sure it feels. I am sure because the first runs already used that split, and because without it an agent here will treat a remembered URL as equal to a file it just opened.

I think confidence should be stated on each load-bearing claim, not only on the file. A file-level grade hides the one weak claim a design decision might rest on. `AGENTS.md` already gestures at this. The standard should make it a requirement, with a fixed vocabulary — high, medium, or low — plus one line of why. I think this is right, with thinner evidence than the points I am sure of: the first runs show why a file-level grade is dangerous, and the GRADE paper records per-finding rating as the method and file-level-only grading as the usual failure.

I am fairly sure that negative results are results. A question investigated and closed as "no evidence" or "not worth the machinery yet" must be recorded, or this estate will buy the same investigation again at full price. I am fairly sure because the first runs already produced one such finding, and `AGENTS.md` had nowhere proper to put it.

I think gathering and synthesis should stay visibly separated in the file — findings versus implications — so a reader can accept the evidence and still reject the inference. I think this is right, with thinner evidence: both first runs landed on that shape without being required to, and two files is a thin sample for a hard rule.

I am not sure a registry of recurring sources is needed. Canonical URLs and verification dates might eventually earn a register. Two files in, that is machinery without a corpus. This is a guess, recorded as an option, not a need.

I think the section shape of a research file already maps onto Diátaxis's documentation modes. Sources and findings carry reference and explanation. Implications carry how-to guidance. The documentation-practice research left a mode taxonomy open because the corpus is small. If a mode taxonomy is ever wanted, applying it at the section level of research files captures the benefit — no mode-mixing inside a section — without a regime that types whole documents, because the required section structure assigns modes for free. I think this is right, with thinner evidence: Diátaxis itself was checked, but this use of it has not been tested in this corpus.

I am fairly sure research depth is a ladder — authoritative lookup, evidence scan, rapid review, systematic review, direct measurement, living review — and that effort should be calibrated to consequence and uncertainty. Calibrate up when a wrong answer is costly or hard to reverse, when sources disagree, when evidence is indirect or interested, or when the conclusion will be widely reused. Calibrate down when the claim is low-consequence, stated by a stable authority, reversible, and independently checkable. A mismatch wastes something either way: rigor on a lookup wastes tokens, and a lookup on a disputed effect wastes trust. I am fairly sure, because the calibration idea is clear and this estate has only exercised the two cheapest rungs.

I am fairly sure corroboration counts independent evidence paths, not URLs. Several pages repeating one upstream source are one source. The check on a load-bearing claim includes tracing it to its origin before counting agreement. That matters here because this repo will otherwise count reprints as confirmation.

I am sure reuse is by reference. A consumer cites the canonical synthesis — an exact revision when reproducibility matters — and never copies it forward. Citation does not promote research into policy. Supersession produces a new file with a durable pointer to the old. That matches the estate's persistence classes, where landed research is a closed dated record. I am sure because a copied-forward synthesis in this repo would become a second home for the same claim, and a citation is not a decision.

## What this suggests for the research standard

These are design inputs, not decisions. The design act weighs them.

1. Keep the five requirements as the core, word for word: the question, the sources, the split between checked facts and believed claims, the confidence, and the date. Every one of them earned its place in the first two files, and nothing we saw argues for dropping any of them.
2. Add a question-first rule. A research file opens with its question. Gathering that happened before the question existed is flagged as such.
3. Name the three source classes and the check each one owes: estate files by reading, external documents by a dated reachability-and-characterization check, memory and inference as claims. Require the verification date on external sources.
4. Require a confidence line on each load-bearing claim, using a fixed vocabulary, not only a grade on the file.
5. Give negative results a named place, so closing a question is as recordable as answering one.
6. Require the findings-versus-implications split, so a reader can accept the evidence without accepting the inference drawn from it.
7. State the lifecycle plainly: research lands as a closed dated record; a later file supersedes it; re-grading changes status metadata only.
8. Note the delegation fit. Gathering may be given to the cheap tier once the question and the source list are fixed. Synthesis and confidence-rating stay with the tier that can be held to judgment.
9. If a documentation-mode taxonomy is ever adopted, apply it at the section level of research files rather than by typing whole documents. The findings-versus-implications split already assigns the modes.
10. Define the depth ladder and the triggers for moving up or down it. Require each research file to name the depth it claims, so a lookup is never dressed as a review and a review is never billed as a lookup.
11. Require the protocol-before-search fields — question, source eligibility, stop conditions — to be stated in the file before gathering. Size them to the depth: one line each at lookup depth.
12. Count corroboration by independent evidence paths. A load-bearing claim is traced to its origins. Repeated downstream copies add nothing.
13. State reuse-by-reference plainly. Consumers cite revisions. Nothing copies research forward. Citation never converts research into policy.

## How sure this is

Fairly sure overall, not certain. The strongest findings are facts of this estate's own first research runs and can be checked by reading the files. The main structural beliefs — fix the question first, and give each source class its own check — rest on a sample of exactly two runs, which is real evidence and thin evidence. Those two beliefs, and the protocol-before-search belief, now also carry the weight of the checked evidence-synthesis discipline (Cochrane, MECIR, and the Kitchenham adaptation). The per-claim confidence position gains external confirmation from the GRADE paper. The source-registry idea is a guess and is marked as one. The Diátaxis section-level idea cites a checked framework, but that application has not been tested in this corpus. The depth ladder adapts a mature discipline's tiers to an estate that has only used the two cheapest rungs.
